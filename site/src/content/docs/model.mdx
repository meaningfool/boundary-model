## Main `boundary` concepts

- **`boundary`**: where a `consumer` meets a `provider`. The `provider` offers its resources and capabilities to the `consumer` across it. There is one `boundary` for each `consumer` and `provider` pair.
- **`interface`**: the channel the `consumer` uses to see resources and use capabilities, such as a web UI or a CLI.
- **`capability`**: something the `consumer` can do with the `provider`'s resources, through one or more interfaces, to get a result.
- **`resource`**: something with a state the `consumer` cares about, such as an order.

## An example: the Store

The Store is an invented online shop that sells home goods. Here is how the `boundary model` describes it, one concept at a time, from the outside in.

### Who uses it

Shoppers buy from the Store. Fraud analysts review the orders it holds, and store managers keep its catalog right. Each is a `consumer` of the same `provider`, the Store, with a `boundary` of its own.

A `consumer` is a role, not a person. Analysts and managers work for the Store, but they use it to get their work done, so they stand outside it, like shoppers.

<Example step="consumers" />

### Through which interfaces

Shoppers reach the Store through a web shop and a mobile app. That makes two interfaces, but still one `boundary`. The `boundary` says what the Shopper can do. An `interface` only says where.

<Example step="interfaces" />

### What they can do

Shoppers come to browse products, place orders and list their past orders. Each is a `capability`: something they do to get a result.

When an order goes through, the Store checks that each product is in stock. That check is a step of Place order, not a `capability`: its only result is whether the order goes ahead.

<Example step="capabilities" />

### What they act on

Capabilities act on resources, the things with a state the Shopper cares about. Browse products reads products, and Place order creates an order and adds loyalty points. A product's price and stock are its properties, details of the product rather than resources of their own.

<Example step="resources" />

### Inside the Store

Open the Store, and the same pattern repeats one level down. To charge an order, Storefront relies on Payments. Storefront is the `consumer`, Payments is the `provider`, and the charges API is the `interface` of the `internal boundary` between them.
